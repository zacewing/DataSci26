Turning lifting logs into answers

A tool that tracks my training and recovery, estimates my strength over time, flags when I've stalled, and uses AI to explain what's working in plain English.

Open the dashboard How it works

The problem

Lifters log numbers but rarely learn from them. It's hard to tell whether a stall is real or just a bad day, what separates good sessions from bad ones, or when a new personal record is realistic.

How it works

1 · Log

Every set (exercise, weight, reps, effort) is entered on the Log page and saved to a CSV file, plus daily sleep, soreness, energy and bodyweight.

2 · Estimate

Each set is converted to an estimated one-rep max, so a 5-rep set and a heavy single can be compared on one scale.

3 · Analyze

Trends, plateau detection and prediction models show where strength is heading and what's driving it.

4 · Explain

An AI model reads the weekly numbers and writes a short summary with suggestions, such as when to push or deload.

Estimating strength

Estimated one-rep max (e1RM) uses the Epley formula:

e1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

Two rules keep the estimate honest:

Singles count as-is

Plain Epley would turn a 315 lb single into ~325. A single already is a one-rep max, so its e1RM is just the weight.

Fatigued sets are left out

A heavy single done after back-off sets makes me look weaker than I am. Those sets still count toward volume, but not toward the strength trend.

Trend, not noise

One bad night of sleep can sink a session. A rolling average over recent sessions shows the real direction.

Build progress

  1. Done
    Load and clean dataReads the CSV logs filled in from the Log tab, fixes typos and inconsistent names, computes e1RM, weekly volume and rolling averages.
  2. Done
    e1RM chartsInteractive trend chart per lift, plus weekly volume.
  3. Done
    Plateau detectionFits a trend line through the last 6 weeks and flags a lift when it gains under 0.5% a week. The first settings, tuned on one scripted lifter, did worse than the simple rule "no PR in 3 weeks" on new lifters (F1 43% vs 52%). Re-tuned on separate lifters, it now scores 56% on 200 lifters it never saw. See the results.
  4. Done
    Prediction modelPredicts next-session e1RM from recent form and recovery. On 120 varied simulated lifters, linear regression's typical miss was 16% smaller than guessing "same as last time" (95% CI 13–19%). Needs about 30 real sessions before it runs on my logs.
  5. Done
    Benchmark on hundreds of liftersEvery model scored on simulated lifters it was never tuned on, against simple baselines, with 95% confidence intervals. Results.
  6. Done
    Real-lifter checkTested on 30,000+ real powerlifters' public meet records (OpenPowerlifting). A flat trend line meant a PR at the next meet only 20% of the time vs. 46% otherwise. Trend lines barely beat "same as last meet" for forecasting. Results.
  7. Started
    What drives good sessionsHow sleep, soreness and time of day relate to performance.
  8. Done
    AI weekly summaryClaude turns the week's numbers into a plain-English check-in, with a rule-based version when no API key is set. Suggestions, not medical advice.
